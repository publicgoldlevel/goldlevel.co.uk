HUMAN-CENTRED CYBERSECURITY UNDER PRESSURE

FA-2026-057·series·3 ordered frames·No. 08 in field
Frame 1 of 3

Summary

A human-centred view of security that includes the person through whom systems are operated. Technical controls matter, while panic, rushed choices, manipulation, unclear boundaries, and broken trust can also become paths of compromise. It holds technology, human pressure, and body within one accountable field.

What it maps

Technology, human pressure, body, breath, feeling, speech, relationship, timing, stewardship, trust, attack surface, and decision quality. The map keeps the interaction between technology and human pressure visible rather than treating either as an isolated variable.

Why it matters

Security programmes fail when they treat people only as weak links. Supporting clear, dignified action under pressure strengthens technical controls and reduces the likelihood that urgency or coercion becomes the attacker’s route. It gives readers a firmer basis for handling technology and human pressure responsibly.
