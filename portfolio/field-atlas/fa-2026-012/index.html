Relational Maturity & Repair

CARE VS CARETAKING

FA-2026-012·series·3 ordered frames·No. 08 in field
Frame 1 of 3

Summary

A three-frame relational distinction between care that remains mutual and caretaking that requires one person to disappear. It examines how generosity can become management when fear, over-responsibility, or conflict avoidance sets the terms. The work keeps reciprocity, self-abandonment, and peacekeeping close to consequence.

What it maps

Reciprocity, self-abandonment, peacekeeping, emotional labour, over-responsibility, and the boundary between offering support and becoming responsible for another person. The field becomes clearer where reciprocity and self-abandonment change pace, role, or consequence.

Why it matters

Care loses its health when the giver must shrink to preserve the bond. Naming the shift helps protect compassion while restoring agency, shared responsibility, and room for honest disagreement. That visibility helps people protect what matters when reciprocity and self-abandonment interact.
