SYSTEMS AND HUMAN JUDGMENT

FA-2026-071·standalone·1 frame·No. 04 in field
Frame 1 of 1

Summary

A compact visual proposition that systems do not sit outside the people who design, interpret, operate, and live with them. Human judgement enters selection, framing, response, and the meaning assigned to outcomes. Its centre is the interaction between systems, judgement, and observer.

What it maps

Systems, judgement, observer, collective life, individual becoming, selection, interpretation, conduct, and the relational field between structure and person. It keeps the transition between systems and judgement visible, especially under pressure.

Why it matters

Treating systems as neutral objects can hide the human choices embedded within them. Keeping judgement visible supports accountability and prevents structure from appearing self-authorising. It helps turn concern about systems and judgement into a more accountable next step.
