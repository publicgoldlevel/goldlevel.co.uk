HUMAN REGULATION — MANY PARTS, ONE HUMAN

FA-2026-066·series·7 ordered frames·No. 01 in fieldPrimary work
Frame 1 of 7

Summary

A visual sequence on human regulation as a coordinated field rather than one isolated symptom or technique. Different parts may carry distinct signals while still belonging to one person whose context and agency remain central.

What it maps

Body, breath, emotion, attention, need, internal parts, coordination, context, agency, and the distinction between useful component maps and the whole person. It follows the points where body and breath meet, diverge, or alter practical choice.

Why it matters

Fragmented frameworks can make people feel like collections of problems. Holding many parts within one human protects dignity and supports reflection without turning the map into diagnosis or treatment. The distinction makes decisions about body and breath easier to examine and revise.
