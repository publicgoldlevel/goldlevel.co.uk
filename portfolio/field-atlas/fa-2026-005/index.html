HUMAN GROUNDING UNDER NEW CONDITIONS

FA-2026-005·series·6 ordered frames·No. 06 in field
Frame 1 of 6

Summary

A six-frame reflection on what remains irreducibly human as technology changes the surface of cognition and work. AI may organise, mirror, or extend capacity, while meaning and responsibility continue to arise through lived experience. It places technological extension, embodiment, and choice inside a shared decision field.

What it maps

Technological extension, embodiment, choice, consequence, relationship, temporal reality, and the difference between cognitive assistance and human responsibility. It includes the feedback loops through which technological extension changes the practical meaning of embodiment.

Why it matters

Fluent systems can make abstraction feel complete. Returning to the body, context, and consequence keeps tools in proportion and protects the human sources of judgement, meaning, and accountability. It supports proportionate action by keeping technological extension and embodiment open to review.
