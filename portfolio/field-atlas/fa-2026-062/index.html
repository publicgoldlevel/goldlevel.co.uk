THE RESPONSIBILITY COLLAPSE PATHWAY

FA-2026-062·series·3 ordered frames·No. 07 in field
Frame 1 of 3

Summary

A public-systems field map focused on ordinary automated and electronic decision pathways rather than hidden-control claims. It asks what happens when power is concentrated in systems while responsibility is scattered among departments, vendors, policies, and nominal review.

What it maps

Automated decisions, scoring, access, responsibility gaps, explanation, data correction, appeal, override, vendor chains, human review, and meaningful stop conditions. The sequence also shows where automated decisions and scoring align, conflict, or require separation.

Why it matters

An institution can look accountable while an affected person has no usable route to challenge harm. Mapping the collapse makes governance testable through explanation, repair, authority, and recourse. The result is a stronger basis for deciding what automated decisions and scoring require.
