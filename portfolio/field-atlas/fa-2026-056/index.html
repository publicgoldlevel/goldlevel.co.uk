THE HOUSE IS NOT AN ENTRANCE

FA-2026-056·series·6 ordered frames·No. 15 in field
Frame 1 of 6

Summary

A boundary sequence using house, gold, echo, mirror, and doorway as symbols of right placement. It argues that what can be seen, copied, quoted, or admired is not therefore available for entry, custody, or ownership.

What it maps

Consent, access, visibility, copying, context, ownership, stewardship, invitation, boundaries, embodiment, and the distinction between public surface and open route. It also tracks how consent and access reinforce or constrain one another over time.

Why it matters

Digital environments often collapse seeing into entitlement. Re-establishing consent and context protects creators and audiences from extraction while keeping boundaries rooted in dignity rather than fear or pursuit. That clarity supports more deliberate choices about consent and access in practice.
