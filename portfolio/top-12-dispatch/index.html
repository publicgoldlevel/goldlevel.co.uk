Three Birmingham questions

Local leads designed to survive reporting.

Each begins as a question, not a current finding. Each requires affected people, responsible actors, records, counterevidence, right of reply and a condition under which the story narrows or stops.

01

Who feeds Birmingham after midnight?

A scene-led question about night workers and late-opening food businesses, tested through hours, prices, location, transport and direct observation.

Reporting lead · verify before publication

02

One ban, many doors

A careful question about how nightlife exclusions are made, recorded, challenged and, where applicable, shared—with privacy, right of reply and legal review built in.

Qualified lead · establish the mechanism

03

Can Birmingham still give you a human answer?

A document-led story following one consequential local decision through the people, policy, records and software behind it.

Ambitious lead · narrow to one service

How the work is carried

From question to reader-facing form.

Define the question, separate evidence, write, edit, make the structure legible, publish accessibly and preserve a route for correction.

Question

State what is being asked and what would narrow it.

Evidence

Separate records, observation, attribution and interpretation.

Writing

Carry a reader through scene, context and consequence.

Visual edit

Use hierarchy and sequence to make complexity legible.

Publishing

Use accessible pages with stable routes and metadata.

Correction

Keep source identity, version and limits visible.

Reader

Explain without asking readers to adopt private terminology.

Boundary

Do not promote polish or archive integrity into evidence.
